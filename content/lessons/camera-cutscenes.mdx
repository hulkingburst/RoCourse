A cutscene is just a camera with a plan. Slide to one framing, hold it, slide to the next — while the player watches and, for a moment, the game runs itself. You already have the two tools: **camera control** from the last lesson and **tweens** for smooth motion. This lesson turns them into shots.

<Step>

## The shot

Every cutscene starts the same way: take control, then tween the camera where you want it.

```luau
local TweenService = game:GetService("TweenService")
local camera = workspace.CurrentCamera
camera.CameraType = Enum.CameraType.Scriptable

local shotInfo = TweenInfo.new(2, Enum.EasingStyle.Quad, Enum.EasingDirection.Out)
local shotGoals = {
    CFrame = CFrame.lookAt(Vector3.new(0, 20, 60), castle.Position),
}

TweenService:Create(camera, shotInfo, shotGoals):Play()
```

Two seconds later the camera has glided from wherever it was to a low, face-on framing of the castle. Scriptable first — otherwise the mouse fights the tween the whole way.

## Chaining shots

One tween is a pan. A cutscene is *several* pans in a row. Chain them with a wait that lets each tween land:

```luau
TweenService:Create(camera, shotOne, goalsOne):Play()
task.wait(2.5) -- give the 2-second tween time to land

TweenService:Create(camera, shotTwo, goalsTwo):Play()
task.wait(2.5)
```

The beat between shots is what lets the motion read. A cutscene is one of the few places a plain `task.wait` is the right tool — nothing else needs to happen while it runs.

## Camera shake

Shake is a tiny random offset applied every frame. Multiply the camera's base CFrame by a small displacement:

```luau
local shakeBase = camera.CFrame

RunService.RenderStepped:Connect(function()
    local offset = Vector3.new(
        math.random(-0.05, 0.05),
        math.random(-0.05, 0.05),
        math.random(-0.05, 0.05)
    )
    camera.CFrame = shakeBase * CFrame.new(offset)
end)
```

- `shakeBase` — the CFrame you shake *around*.
- `* CFrame.new(offset)` — CFrame multiplication composes transforms: the base, nudged a few centimeters in a random direction, every frame.
- The smaller the offset, the subtler the shake.

## The FOV punch

Widen the lens and the world rushes toward the player — the classic zoom punch when a boss lands or a bomb goes off. `FieldOfView` is a property, so it tweenable like anything else:

```luau
camera.FieldOfView = 70 -- the normal lens

TweenService:Create(camera, punchInfo, { FieldOfView = 95 }):Play()
```

A quick tween up to 95 makes the hit *feel* like it hits. Tween it back to 70 for the recovery.

## The wrap

A cutscene that never ends is a locked player. When the scene is over, hand the controls back:

```luau
camera.CameraType = Enum.CameraType.Custom
```

`Custom` returns the camera to the engine — character follows, mouse looks, back to normal.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to hand control back.** Leave it Scriptable and the player is trapped in your framing forever. `CameraType.Custom` on the way out.
- **Shake and cutscene fighting.** A shake loop and a cutscene tween both assigning `camera.CFrame` stomp each other. End the shake before the tween — or shake the base you tween.
- **Cutscenes on the server.** The camera is per-player and client-side. Run the scene in a LocalScript and trigger it with a RemoteEvent if the server decides *when*.
- **Waiting too little between shots.** A 2-second tween with a 0.1-second wait gets chopped off. Give the tween its time to land.
