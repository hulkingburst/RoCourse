Before anything else, you need the tool you'll use for the whole course. Roblox Studio is the free app where games are built.

<Step>

## Download and sign in

1. Go to the Roblox website and sign in (create an account if you don't have one — you'll need it to build, test, and publish).
2. Find the **Create** or **Studio** button in the top bar.
3. Download and run the installer. Studio downloads itself on first launch — be patient, it's a big app the first time.

When Studio opens, sign in with the same account. Studio remembers you, so this is usually once per machine. If you can see your profile in the corner, you're in.

<Note>
Studio is a desktop app. The website is where you manage your account and published games; Studio is where you actually build.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step>

## Start from a Baseplate

Studio opens on a home page with a grid of templates. You want **Baseplate** — a flat gray platform floating in space. It's the cleanest starting point: empty world, solid floor, nothing else.

1. Click the **Baseplate** template.
2. Wait for it to load. You'll see the 3D view with the gray baseplate in the middle.

Press **Ctrl+S** (Cmd+S on Mac) and save it as something like `LearningProject`. Studio saves places to the cloud under your account.

<Expandable title="What is a 'place' exactly?">
A place is one Roblox game world — one buildable space. "Place" is the official word for what you're building. Your LearningProject is a place, and it's where every lesson will happen.
</Expandable>

</Step>

<Step>

## Your workspace is ready

Close Studio, reopen it, and open `LearningProject` again from the home page. That round trip — close, reopen, find your place — is how you'll open your work every day from now on.
