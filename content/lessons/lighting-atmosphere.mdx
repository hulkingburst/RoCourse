A flat white workspace feels like a test room. Lighting and atmosphere give your world depth, mood, and time of day — sunrise, night, fog over a swamp, bloom on neon signs. These aren't just decoration; they're the first thing players notice.

<Step>

## Time of day

The `Lighting` service controls the global light:

```luau
local Lighting = game:GetService("Lighting")

Lighting.ClockTime = 14   -- 2:00 PM (0 = midnight, 12 = noon)
Lighting.GeographicLatitude = 0 -- angle of the sun
```

`ClockTime` is the simplest lever — a number from 0 to 24 that sets the sun position. 6 is sunrise, 12 is noon, 18 is sunset, 0 is midnight.

## Sky and Atmosphere

A skybox and atmosphere add realistic depth:

```luau
local sky = Instance.new("Sky")
sky.SunTextureId = "rbxassetid://0"  -- default sun
sky.MoonTextureId = "rbxassetid://0" -- default moon
sky.StarCount = 3000
sky.Parent = Lighting

local atmosphere = Instance.new("Atmosphere")
atmosphere.Density = 0.3    -- fog thickness (0 = clear, 1 = thick)
atmosphere.Color = Color3.fromRGB(200, 220, 255)  -- tint
atmosphere.Haze = 2         -- haze amount
atmosphere.Parent = Lighting
```

- `Density` controls how much the horizon fades into the sky — low values for clear days, high for foggy swamps.
- `Haze` adds a warm or cool haze layer.
- The `Sky` object renders the skybox behind everything.

Together they turn a gray void into a sky with clouds, a sun, and realistic horizon fading.

</Step>

<Step>

## Fog for depth

Built-in fog hides distant objects and adds atmosphere:

```luau
Lighting.FogEnd = 1000     -- objects beyond 1000 studs fade to fog color
Lighting.FogColor = Color3.fromRGB(180, 200, 220)
```

`FogEnd` is the distance where objects are fully fogged. For a horror game, set it to 50 — the player can barely see. For an open world, set it to 5000 for subtle depth.

<Note>
Atmosphere and FogStackingDensity work together — Atmosphere gives realistic sky-based fog, while FogEnd gives a simple distance cutoff. Use one or both.
</Note>

</Step>

## PostEffects for polish

PostEffects add camera-level visual polish:

```luau
local bloom = Instance.new("BloomEffect")
bloom.Intensity = 0.5     -- glow strength
bloom.Size = 24           -- glow spread
bloom.Threshold = 0.8     -- brightness cutoff
bloom.Parent = Lighting

local colorCorrection = Instance.new("ColorCorrectionEffect")
colorCorrection.Brightness = 0.02
colorCorrection.Contrast = 0.1
colorCorrection.Saturation = 0.2
colorCorrection.Parent = Lighting
```

- **BloomEffect** — makes bright things glow. Perfect for neon signs, magic effects, and sun glare.
- **ColorCorrectionEffect** — adjusts overall brightness, contrast, and saturation. Warm up a sunset, desaturate a horror scene.

## Animating a day/night cycle

A simple day/night loop:

```luau
while true do
    for t = 6, 20, 0.1 do  -- sunrise to sunset
        Lighting.ClockTime = t
        task.wait(0.05)
    end
    for t = 20, 30, 0.1 do  -- sunset to next sunrise (wraps past 24)
        Lighting.ClockTime = t % 24
        task.wait(0.05)
    end
end
```

This smoothly advances the sun across the sky. Combine with Atmosphere density changes (thicker fog at dawn, clear at noon) for a more dynamic feel.

## Common mistakes

- **Setting FogEnd too low by accident.** 50 studs means the player can't see anything. Default is 1000000 — only lower it intentionally.
- **Too many PostEffects.** Bloom + ColorCorrection + SunRays + Blur together is expensive. Pick one or two that match the mood.
- **Forgetting Atmosphere.** A Sky alone doesn't fade the horizon — you need Atmosphere to blend the sky into distant terrain.
- **Animating ClockTime in a tight loop with no wait.** The sun jumps instead of moving smoothly. Always `task.wait()` between updates.
