You've published. Now: who gets in? Permissions decide that, and they're the difference between a safe test with friends and strangers wandering into a half-built world.

<Step>

## Who can play

Game Settings → **Permissions** controls the door:

- **Public** — anyone on Roblox can play. Your game is discoverable.
- **Friends** — only your friends (and people they invite) can play.
- **Private** — effectively only people you specifically allow.

The safe order: build Friends → test → when the game is solid, widen to Public. You can change this any time.

## Getting the link to testers

Publishing ≠ discovered. To get testers quickly:

1. Open your experience on the Roblox website.
2. Copy the **experience link** from the page.
3. Send it to your friends — desktop players can also search the experience name.

The link is the quickest path from "I built this" to "someone else is playing it." You'll send it a lot while testing.

<Note>
With permissions set to Friends, the link only works for friends — exactly what you want during a bug hunt.
</Note>

## Private servers and test servers

Two terms worth knowing:

- **Private servers** — paid/owned copies of your game where only invited people play. Great for tournaments and group practice; not needed yet.
- **Dev/Test servers** — professional developers run these to test changes without touching the live game. When your game has a real audience, this becomes your safety net.

For now, Friends permissions + a link is the whole testing loop.
