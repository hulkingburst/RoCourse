The camera is your player's view of the world — and by default, Roblox drives it. The character follows, the mouse looks around, all automatic. **Custom cameras take the wheel:** you decide exactly where the view sits and where it points. Cutscenes, boss-intro pan shots, locked-camera puzzles, first-person views — they all start with the same five lines.

<Step>

## Meet the camera

Every player has one camera, and you get it from Workspace:

```luau
local camera = workspace.CurrentCamera
```

- `CurrentCamera` — the active camera for *this* player.
- The camera is **per-player and client-side**, so camera code lives in a **LocalScript** in `StarterPlayerScripts`. One player zooming to a tower must never tilt someone else's view.

## Taking the wheel: Scriptable

The engine ignores your CFrame assignments as long as it's in charge. Switch it off:

```luau
local camera = workspace.CurrentCamera

camera.CameraType = Enum.CameraType.Scriptable
camera.CFrame = CFrame.new(0, 30, 40)
```

- `CameraType.Scriptable` — "a script owns the camera now."
- `camera.CFrame` — where the camera stands *and* which way it faces.

From this moment the mouse and the character no longer move the camera. You set the CFrame, the camera obeys.

## Aiming: CFrame.lookAt

`CFrame.new(0, 30, 40)` puts the camera high above the world — but it keeps whatever direction it was already facing. To *point* it, use `lookAt`:

```luau
camera.CFrame = CFrame.lookAt(Vector3.new(0, 30, 40), castle.Position)
```

Two arguments: **where the camera stands**, then **what it looks at**. If the target moves, the camera keeps staring at its old spot — which is why real camera scripts re-aim every frame (next step).

## The follow cam

Aim once and a moving target walks out of frame. The fix: re-aim **every frame**. `RenderStepped` fires right before the game renders — the correct home for camera math:

```luau
local RunService = game:GetService("RunService")
local camera = workspace.CurrentCamera
local target = workspace.Coin

RunService.RenderStepped:Connect(function()
    camera.CFrame = CFrame.lookAt(camera.CFrame.Position, target.Position)
end)
```

`camera.CFrame.Position` — "where the camera already stands." Pass that plus the moving target, and the camera swings to follow the coin however it moves.

<Note>
RenderStepped runs on the client before every rendered frame. That's where smooth camera work goes — a `while true` loop with `task.wait` is too chunky for a camera.
</Note>

## Common mistakes

- **Writing camera code in a Script.** The camera is your player's personal view — client-side, in a LocalScript. A server Script touching it is both wrong and pointless.
- **Forgetting CFrame carries direction.** `camera.CFrame = CFrame.new(0, 30, 40)` teleports the camera but keeps the old facing. Point it with `lookAt`.
- **Two writers fighting over CFrame.** A follow loop and a cutscene tween both assigning `camera.CFrame` = flicker. One camera, one writer at a time.
- **Never giving control back.** A Scriptable camera stays locked until you say otherwise. Next lesson shows how to hand the mouse back.
